## Memory Systems - Part 1
### Memory Formation

Core Question:

How does a system decide what deserves to be remembered?

---

Observation:

Importance is not a property of information.

Importance depends on future usefulness.

---

Example:

"Alice lives in Paris"

feels important because it can be useful later.

---

"The music was playing"

usually has low future usefulness.

---

Challenge:

When information arrives,

the model does not know the future.

It does not know what will be needed later.

---

Memory Formation Problem:

For every piece of information:

Should I store it?

Or discard it?

---

Naive Solution 1:

Store Everything.

Problem:

Memory grows forever.

Not scalable.

---

Naive Solution 2:

Store Nothing.

Problem:

No persistence.

No memory.

---

Key Insight:

Memory is fundamentally a compression problem.

---

Humans do not remember every word.

Humans remember meaning.

---

Goal:

Convert large history

into

small useful memories.

---

Researchers noticed different kinds of information:

Transient Information

Useful briefly.

Example:

Music was playing.

---

Persistent Information

Useful much later.

Example:

Alice lives in Paris.

---

Relational Information

Connects entities and facts.

Example:

Alice works at Google.

---

Main Insight:

Memory is selective compression of useful information.

---

Raw text?
